      Empirical Findings & Directional Behavior

      #1 Dominant Feature

      Uncommon Top-Level Domains (is_common_tld)

      As observed in the paper's beeswarm plot (Fig. 2), is_common_tld has the highest directional impact. Links hosted on cheap or exotic TLDs (.top, .xyz, .click, .buzz) heavily shift the model toward the phishing class, whereas trusted ccTLDs (.sa, .ma, .ae) shift it toward legitimate.

      #2 Dominant Feature

      Shannon Entropy & Randomness

      Cybercriminals frequently use Domain Generation Algorithms (DGA) or arbitrary hashes. Higher Shannon entropy in domain names directly correlates with high positive SHAP values (malicious intent).

      #3 Crucial Counter-Intuitive Insight

      The HTTPS Fallacy (has_https)

      The SHAP importance of has_https is near zero in modern datasets. Phishers now obtain free SSL certificates (Let's Encrypt, Cloudflare) on over 75% of fraudulent advertisement landing pages. Relying on the "padlock icon" alone is no longer safe.
