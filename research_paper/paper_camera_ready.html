Detecting Fraudulent URLs on Social Platforms Using an Explainable Machine Learning Approach

SK. Thahir*, V. Mahesh, T. Lokesh, CH. Bhanu
Department of Computer Science & Information Technology, Hassan First University / Autonomous Engineering College
Abstract
Online advertisements are increasingly used to spread phishing URLs on Arabic social media platforms. This study aims to detect malicious URLs targeting social media platforms by building the Social Media Phishing URL Dataset, which contains 2,400 balanced URLs, where 1,200 are malicious URLs and the rest are legitimate. Moreover, this research compares the performance of machine learning models, namely Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, and SVM, with a Deep Neural Network model. Furthermore, this research evaluates the decisions of the proposed model using the SHAP method. This study develops an explainable XGBoost model capable of detecting malicious URLs used in online advertisements on social platforms, achieving an accuracy of 96.88% and an F1-score of 96.82%. The results show that some features, such as uncommon top-level domains, increase the probability of classifying a URL as malicious. These findings provide valuable insights that can help users better identify malicious URLs, thereby enhancing their awareness and protection against phishing attacks.

1. Introduction

Phishing is a type of financial cybercrime [1] where cybercriminals attempt to gain an illegal access into individuals sensitive information by tricking them into giving their login credentials, or financial data [2,3]. phishing, attackers send messages that appear to originate from trusted entities such as banks, employers, or service providers. Smishing relies on SMS messages, often containing urgent or alarming requests, to deceive users into taking immediate action. In vishing, attackers impersonate legitimate organizations during phone calls in order to manipulate victims into revealing sensitive information. Another common form of phishing occurs through online advertising on social media platforms [4], where cybercriminals post fake job offers [5], promote fraudulent prize-winning schemes [6], or impersonate well-known e-commerce platforms by offering high-value products at unrealistically low prices [7]. The common characteristic shared by these different forms of phishing is the use of malicious Uniform Resource Locators (URLs) [8].

Malicious URLs are used to distribute harmful content. By clicking on these links, users may unintentionally download malware that can compromise their devices or be deceived into disclosing sensitive personal information [9]. Additionally, such URLs may function as landing pages that redirect users to contact scammers directly through messaging platforms such as WhatsApp or Telegram. These types of attacks constitute a significant hindrance to the growth of e-commerce and undermine consumer trust in the use of digital services, particularly online banking.

Given the severe financial and privacy consequences of such attacks [2], the accurate detection and analysis of malicious URLs has emerged as a critical area of research [10]. Machine learning and deep learning techniques have significantly improved malicious URL detection compared to traditional blacklists and whitelists approaches, which are unable to keep pace with the increasing volume and diversity of attacks [2]. The prevalence of online phishing campaigns targeting Arabic-language social media platforms has been steadily increasing [4]. Notably, new malicious URLs have emerged that are tailored to regional cultural contexts, employ localized domain names, and attempt to impersonate prominent companies in regional countries. This trend highlights the urgent need for adaptive detection methods capable of identifying such threats. Despite this, studies focusing specifically on URLs targeting regional users remain scarce.

This study aims to detect malicious URLs targeting social media platforms. To achieve this, a dataset was constructed containing malicious URLs used in online advertisements on these platforms. Furthermore, the study evaluates and compares the performance of several machine learning models, namely Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and Support Vector Machine (SVM), alongside a Deep Neural Network model. In addition, this research interprets the decisions of the proposed model using the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) method. The rest of the paper is organized as follows: Section 2 provides a detailed explanation of the methodology, Section 3 presents the results and discussion, and Section 4 concludes the study.

2. Methodology

2.1. Arabic Social Media Phishing URL Dataset

This research built the Social Media Phishing URL Dataset, which contains 2,400 balanced URLs, with 1,200 malicious and 1,200 benign. The malicious URLs were collected manually from active phishing advertisements across Meta platforms. For advertisements claiming prizes or monetary rewards through lotteries, we traced the links to their landing pages and confirmed they were phishing sites intended to harvest users' personal information; these URLs, along with others of the same type, were labeled as malicious. In the case of fake job advertisements, we engaged with the advertisers as potential applicants, and once the fraudulent nature was verified, these ads and all similar ones were also classified as malicious.

Legitimate URLs were collected from official government portals, banking institutions, e-commerce platforms, energy companies, and media websites commonly used by regional users. Only verified and trusted domains were considered, and no URL-shortening services were included without full destination unmasking. We ensured diversity by collecting URLs with different paths and parameters.

2.2. Feature extraction

This study extracted a set of features from the dataset that capture different aspects of the URL, including its structure, content, and statistical properties. These features were selected based on the literature. Basic URL characteristics include the overall length of the URL (url_length), the number of digits (num_digits), the number of special characters (num_special_chars), and structural elements such as the number of slashes (num_slashes), query parameters (num_params), and fragments (num_fragments). The number of subdomains (num_subdomains) was also computed, as malicious URLs often use multiple subdomains to mimic legitimate websites. Security-related features were incorporated, such as the presence of HTTPS (has_https) and whether the URL was shortened (is_shortened_url) using common URL shorteners. This study also included features detecting suspicious words (has_suspicious_words) and their frequency (suspicious_word_count) in the URL. For instance, words like login, verify, banking, and lottery are often used in malicious campaigns. Additionally, features such as repeated characters (repeated_chars) and hexadecimal sequences (has_hex) were extracted, as these often indicate obfuscation or attempts to hide malicious content.

Furthermore, the domain name and its top-level domain (TLD) were extracted to compute the length of the TLD (tld_length) and whether the TLD was commonly used (is_common_tld) in legitimate websites, such as .com, .edu, or country-specific domains like .ma and .sa. The presence of an IP address instead of a domain name (has_ip) was also considered, as phishing URLs sometimes use direct IP addresses to bypass domain-based detection. Path-level features capture the complexity of the URL beyond the domain. The number of directories in the path (num_directories) and the total path length (path_length) were computed, as excessively long or deeply nested paths can be indicative of malicious intent. Moreover shannon entropy (entropy) was computed for the domain name to quantify its randomness. Shannon entropy measures the unpredictability of a string, higher entropy indicates more randomness [11]. The ratio of letters to digits (letter_digit_ratio), was also calculated to capture imbalances in the composition of the URL. These extracted features were then used as input for machine learning models.

2.3. Model Selection and Metrics Evaluation

To classify URLs as malicious or legitimate, this research compared the performance of several machine learning models, namely Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and XGBoost, alongside Logistic Regression, SVM, and a Deep Neural Network model. The choice of tree-based models was motivated by the nature of the data, as models such as XGBoost have been shown to outperform deep learning models on tabular datasets [12,13]. The models were evaluated using standard performance metrics, namely the Accuracy, Recall, Precision, and F1-Score.

2.4. Explainability

To provide explanations for the predictions of our best-performing model, this research applied the SHAP method. SHAP is based on the Shapley value, a concept from cooperative game theory [14], which quantifies the contribution of each feature to the model's output. By calculating these contributions, SHAP shows clearly how each feature affects whether a URL is predicted to be malicious or legitimate, providing a deeper understanding of the model's decision-making process.

3.2. Explainability

To examine the predictions made by the XGBoost model, the SHAP method was applied. Figure 1 presents the SHAP summary plot for the XGBoost model, showing the mean absolute SHAP values of the extracted URL features, and Figure 2 displays a beeswarm plot.

The results indicate that features related to the domain dominate the model's decision-making process. In particular, is_common_tld and tld_length emerge as the most influential predictors, highlighting the importance of top-level domain characteristics in distinguishing phishing URLs from legitimate ones. The entropy of the domain also shows a strong contribution. Structural features such as letter_digit_ratio, num_slashes, num_subdomains, and path_length appear in the mid-range of the importance ranking, confirming that URL structural complexity also is important. The rest of the features, such as has_suspicious_words and is_shortened_url, have a little contribution to the model's decisions.

Fig. 2 Illustration of the SHAP beeswarm plot

The beeswarm plot shows that is_common_tld exhibits a clear directional behavior, as uncommon TLDs, which correspond to low values of this feature, push the model's predictions toward the phishing class, whereas common TLDs shift the model's decision toward classifying the URL as legitimate. Moreover, higher values of tld_length and entropy are strongly associated with positive SHAP contributions. Hence, long and highly random domain names are classified as malicious URLs by the model. Structural features such as letter_digit_ratio, num_slashes, num_subdomains, and path_length display clear patterns, where higher values generally correspond to higher SHAP values. This pattern confirms that URLs with deep directory structures, multiple subdomains, and abnormal character distributions systematically increase the likelihood of URLs being classified as malicious, while simpler URLs tend to be classified as legitimate. Lower-ranked features such as has_https, repeated_chars, and has_ip have SHAP values close to zero, indicating limited influence across most samples.

The low contribution of features such as has_https and has_ip indicates that cybercriminals are currently creating malicious URLs that use HTTPS. Moreover, URLs containing IP addresses are relatively scarce in attacks observed on social media platforms. These findings indicate that explainability methods such as SHAP not only help explain the model's decisions but may also help users gain insight into how the model classifies malicious URLs, thereby providing a first layer of protection against phishing attacks.

4. Conclusion

Malicious URLs are a threat to the growth of e-commerce as well as the use of digital services such as online banking. This study proposed an explainable machine learning model to detect malicious URLs used in online advertisements on social media platforms, using an expanded dataset of 2,400 samples. The XGBoost model achieved a superior performance of 96.88% accuracy, 98.28% precision, and an F1-score of 96.82%. To investigate the model's predictions, this study employed the SHAP method. The findings offer useful insights that can assist users in recognizing malicious URLs.

Future work will focus on integrating real-time browser extension defenses with on-device machine learning. We also intend to investigate how large language models can enhance the detection of URLs based on textual and visual features within online advertisements.
