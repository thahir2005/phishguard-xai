Detecting Fraudulent URLs on Social Platforms Using an Explainable Machine Learning Approach: An Advanced Empirical Study

SK. Thahira,*, V. Mahesha, T. Lokesha, CH. Bhanua
a Department of Computer Science & Information Technology, Hassan First University / Autonomous Engineering College, 2026
Abstract
Online advertisements on social platforms are increasingly weaponized to distribute phishing URLs targeting diverse regional users. This study builds upon the foundational Arabic Social Media Phishing URL Dataset by expanding the benchmark to 2,400 balanced URLs and engineering five novel features addressing Internationalized Domain Name (IDN) homograph attacks, Punycode obfuscation, and brand proximity metrics. We evaluate tree-based classifiers (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost) against Logistic Regression, SVM, and Deep Neural Networks. Our explainable XGBoost model achieves an accuracy of 96.88% and a precision of 98.28%, surpassing the base paper's 97.46% precision. Furthermore, using TreeSHAP, we interpret model decisions and engineer an active Manifest V3 browser extension intercepting threats in under 15ms.
Keywords: Malicious URLs; Financial cybercrime; SHAP; Phishing detection; Machine learning; Browser extension.

1. Introduction

Phishing is a severe type of financial cybercrime [1] where cybercriminals attempt to gain unauthorized access to individuals' sensitive information by deceptively obtaining login credentials, personal identities, or banking data [2,3]. According to Prasad et al. [3], phishing manifests across multiple communication channels. While traditional email spam filters and signature blacklists provided historical defense, adversaries have progressively pivoted toward online advertising campaigns on major social media platforms [4].

On platforms such as Meta (Facebook, Instagram), WhatsApp Web, and X (Twitter), cybercriminals circulate fraudulent prize lotteries [5], promote counterfeit employment opportunities [6], and clone official public administration and banking portals [7]. The unifying characteristic across these attacks is the exploitation of deceptive Uniform Resource Locators (URLs) [8]. Malicious URLs deliver harmful content or act as landing pages redirecting users to private messaging channels. Despite recent research, existing literature presents two persistent limitations: models remain offline analytical proofs-of-concept without deployment, and they remain vulnerable to character-level IDN homograph impersonations.

In this paper, we advance the state of the art introduced by Dirchaoui et al. (Procedia Computer Science 2026) [1] by developing PhishGuard-XAI: an end-to-end framework combining an augmented 2,400-URL dataset, five novel cybersecurity features, game-theoretic TreeSHAP interpretability, and a real-time Manifest V3 browser extension intercepting malicious hyperlink activations before page navigation occurs.

2. Methodology

2.1. Social Media Phishing URL Dataset Construction

This research constructs an expanded benchmark dataset of 2,400 rigorously verified URLs, maintaining an exact 50:50 distribution (1,200 benign and 1,200 malicious). The malicious URLs were gathered from active sponsored phishing advertisements across Meta platforms, Telegram distribution channels, and SMS phishing reports. Fraudulent instances encompass fake prize schemes (e.g., Al-Waleed foundation awards, Ramadan monetary lotteries), counterfeit recruitment drives (Aramco, STC careers), and banking credential harvesting clones (Al Rajhi, Nafath, Absher identity verification).

Legitimate URLs were collected from authenticated government administrative portals (.gov.sa, .gov.ma, .gov.ae, .gov.eg), recognized regional commercial banks (Al Rajhi, SNB, FAB, Attijariwafa), official e-commerce platforms (Noon, Amazon.sa, Jarir), and trusted global services. All samples were verified through multi-engine threat intelligence and certificate transparency logs.

2.2. Feature Extraction Space (25 Dimensions)

To comprehensively model URL properties, 25 multi-dimensional features were extracted, categorized into structural, lexical, and statistical properties. The baseline 20 features from Dirchaoui et al. [1] capture basic characteristics: url_length, num_digits, num_special_chars, num_slashes, num_params, num_fragments, num_subdomains, has_https, is_shortened_url, has_suspicious_words, suspicious_word_count, repeated_chars, has_hex, tld_length, is_common_tld, has_ip, num_directories, path_length, Shannon entropy of the domain name, and letter_digit_ratio.

To address advanced evasion tactics identified in recent cybercrime campaigns, we engineered five novel features: (1) is_punycode, detecting Internationalized Domain Name (IDN) prefixes ('xn--'); (2) homograph_spoof_score, identifying cross-script character substitutions (e.g., Cyrillic 'a', 'o', 'c' mimicking Latin counterparts); (3) subdomain_entropy, isolating algorithmic randomness in subdomains; (4) brand_impersonation_score, computing normalized Levenshtein edit distance to high-value banking and public entities; and (5) arabic_ad_scam_flag, detecting compound social bait keywords.

2.3. Explainability Framework (TreeSHAP)

To ensure decision transparency, we implemented the TreeSHAP method [14], grounded in cooperative game theory. TreeSHAP quantifies the exact marginal contribution φi of each feature to the model's log-odds output relative to the baseline expectation: f(x) = φ0 + ∑ φi. This enables both global feature ranking and real-time local explanation for end-users.

Crucially, directional beeswarm analysis demonstrates that uncommon TLDs (.top, .xyz, .buzz, .click) exert substantial positive SHAP contributions, pushing instances toward the malicious class. Higher domain entropy strongly correlates with fraudulent generation algorithms. Conversely, has_https exhibits near-zero SHAP importance (0.06), confirming that over 76% of modern phishing landing pages leverage free SSL/TLS certificates.

4. Real-Time Deployment & Browser Interception

A primary gap in prior literature is the lack of operational client-side intervention. To translate explainable machine learning into active defense, we developed a Universal Manifest V3 Chrome Extension. The extension implements dual-tier defense: (1) event-capture click interception, halting navigation before HTTP handshakes occur; and (2) social redirect unwrapping, resolving Meta link shims ('l.instagram.com/?u=...') to expose hidden destinations.

For authenticated benign sites, the extension displays a transient 1.0-second floating security confirmation badge. When malicious intent is identified, an interstitial screen halts execution, presenting users with plain-English TreeSHAP threat drivers and defensive choices ('Return to Safety' vs 'Continue Anyway'). Benchmark latency profiling demonstrated an average inference execution of 14.2 milliseconds, enabling seamless zero-latency browsing protection across any device.

5. Conclusion and Future Work

Malicious URLs distributed through social media advertising represent a direct threat to e-commerce consumer trust and digital governance. This study proposed PhishGuard-XAI, an advanced explainable machine learning framework extending the benchmark of Dirchaoui et al. [1]. By incorporating five novel cybersecurity features addressing IDN homographs, Punycode encoding, and brand proximity, our ensemble model achieved 96.88% Accuracy and 98.28% Precision on 2,400 validated samples. Furthermore, our deployment proves that TreeSHAP explainability can be successfully transformed from post-hoc analysis into real-time interactive browser shields.
