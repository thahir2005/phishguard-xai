PhishGuard-XAI: An Explainable Machine Learning Framework and Real-Time Browser Defense Against Fraudulent URLs on Social Platforms

SK. Thahir*, V. Mahesh, T. Lokesh, CH. Bhanu
Department of Computer Science & Information Technology, Hassan First University / Autonomous Engineering College
Abstract
Fraudulent advertisements distributed through social platforms represent an escalating vector of credential theft and financial cybercrime. While foundational research by Dirchaoui et al. established benchmark machine learning models for detecting phishing URLs on Arabic social platforms, existing methodologies remain offline proofs-of-concept vulnerable to Internationalized Domain Name (IDN) homograph obfuscation and social redirect shims. In this study, we propose PhishGuard-XAI, an advanced cyber defense framework trained on an expanded, balanced dataset of 2,400 verified URLs. We engineer a 25-dimensional feature space integrating five novel cybercrime indicators: Punycode parsing, homograph substitution scoring, subdomain entropy, brand edit distance proximity, and compound social lure heuristics. Evaluated across standard baseline architectures (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, SVM, and Deep Neural Networks), our proposed gradient-boosted ensemble achieves 96.88% Accuracy and 98.28% Precision, surpassing the baseline XGBoost benchmark of 97.46%. Using game-theoretic TreeSHAP, we interpret feature attributions and translate these explanations into an autonomous Manifest V3 browser extension delivering sub-15ms client-side threat interception.

1. Introduction

The proliferation of digital banking and social commerce has coincided with a dramatic rise in targeted financial cybercrime [1]. Modern adversaries have transitioned away from crude email spam toward sophisticated sponsored advertising campaigns on major social media networks, including Meta (Facebook, Instagram), X (Twitter), and WhatsApp Web [2,3]. These sponsored campaigns exploit algorithmic trust by circulating counterfeit employment opportunities, fraudulent welfare lotteries, and cloned public identity authentication portals [4,5].

The linchpin of these fraudulent operations is the deceptive Uniform Resource Locator (URL) [6]. Malicious URLs serve as high-converting conduits that either execute silent drive-by payload downloads or direct victims toward credential-harvesting landing pages [7]. Despite the emergence of machine learning classifiers for automated URL detection, the foundational work of Dirchaoui et al. [1] and related studies [8,9] highlight three critical limitations in the current literature: (1) existing models operate strictly in post-hoc, offline batch settings without operational client-side integration; (2) feature spaces fail to address character-level homograph spoofing and Punycode obfuscation; and (3) social platform link-wrapping shims (e.g., Meta's 'l.instagram.com/?u=...') frequently blind classifiers to underlying malicious destinations.

To resolve these operational and algorithmic bottlenecks, this paper introduces PhishGuard-XAI, an end-to-end explainable cyber defense system. Our primary contributions are fourfold: (1) we construct an expanded, balanced dataset of 2,400 verified URLs representing modern social engineering campaigns; (2) we formulate a 25-dimensional feature extraction space introducing five novel structural and lexical metrics; (3) we achieve state-of-the-art precision (98.28%), which is critical in operational cybersecurity to prevent disruptive false alarms on legitimate public portals; and (4) we operationalize the TreeSHAP explainability engine into a universal Manifest V3 browser extension that evaluates incoming navigation links in under 15 milliseconds.

2. Methodology and System Architecture

2.1. Social Media Phishing URL Dataset Expansion

Building upon the foundational 1,600-sample Arabic Social Media Phishing URL Dataset established by Dirchaoui et al. [1], we expanded the benchmark to 2,400 verified URLs with an exact 50:50 distribution (1,200 malicious and 1,200 benign). Malicious samples were harvested from active sponsored ad campaigns on Meta platforms, fraudulent Telegram broadcast groups, and confirmed SMS phishing feeds. The malicious corpus spans three prevalent categories: (a) government credential harvesting (clones of Absher, Nafath, and regional public welfare initiatives); (b) banking credential harvesting (spoofs of Al Rajhi, SNB, Attijariwafa, and FAB); and (c) lottery and recruitment frauds (counterfeit Aramco hiring drives, fake gift subsidies, and crypto lotteries).

Legitimate samples were curated from authenticated public administrative domains (.gov.sa, .gov.ma, .gov.ae, .gov.eg), recognized commercial banks, regional e-commerce platforms (Noon, Amazon.sa, Jarir), and global collaboration tools (Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet). All URLs were cross-verified via multi-engine threat intelligence feeds and certificate transparency logs. Fully resolved destination URLs were extracted to eliminate benign shortener bias.

2.2. Novel 25-Dimensional Feature Engineering Space

The feature extraction pipeline computes 25 attributes categorized into structural, lexical, statistical, and brand-proximity dimensions. We retain the 20 foundational features from Dirchaoui et al. [1]: url_length, num_digits, num_special_chars, num_slashes, num_params, num_fragments, num_subdomains, has_https, is_shortened_url, has_suspicious_words, suspicious_word_count, repeated_chars, has_hex, tld_length, is_common_tld, has_ip, num_directories, path_length, domain Shannon entropy, and letter_digit_ratio.

To counter modern adversarial evasion techniques, we engineered five novel features: (1) is_punycode, a binary flag detecting Internationalized Domain Name (IDN) prefixes ('xn--'); (2) homograph_spoof_score, measuring character-level visual lookalikes using Cyrillic and Greek Unicode mappings that visually imitate Latin banking characters; (3) subdomain_entropy, isolating Shannon randomness within subdomains to detect dynamic domain generation algorithms (DGA); (4) brand_impersonation_score, calculating normalized Levenshtein edit distance against a dictionary of high-value regional and international banking institutions; and (5) arabic_ad_scam_flag, detecting compound social bait keywords (e.g., 'wazifa', 'tasjeel', 'fawz', 'ribh').

2.3. Model Selection and Evaluation Framework

To rigorously benchmark performance, we trained and evaluated six classification architectures: Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), Gradient Boosting (GB), Support Vector Machines (SVM with RBF kernel), a Deep Neural Network (DNN: 3 hidden layers with ReLU and Dropout), and eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost). The 2,400-sample dataset was partitioned into an 80% training set (1,920 samples) and a 20% independent test set (480 samples) using stratified sampling to preserve class equilibrium. Hyperparameters were tuned via 5-fold cross-validation. Performance was evaluated using standard statistical metrics: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-Score.

2.4. Game-Theoretic Explainability (TreeSHAP)

To ensure decision transparency in operational cybersecurity, we integrated the TreeSHAP framework [10], rooted in cooperative game theory. TreeSHAP computes the exact Shapley value φi for each feature, defining its additive contribution to the model's log-odds output relative to the expected baseline: f(x) = φ0 + ∑ φi. This formulation guarantees local accuracy, missingness, and consistency, allowing human security analysts and end-users to understand precisely why a given hyperlink was blocked or approved.

As established in Table 1, our proposed PhishGuard-XAI framework achieves 96.88% Accuracy and 98.28% Precision, surpassing the baseline XGBoost model's 97.46% precision reported by Dirchaoui et al. In practical cyber defense, precision is the decisive operational metric: a high false-positive rate causes 'alert fatigue' and blocks users from accessing critical government portals or legitimate webinar platforms. On the 480 test samples, our model yielded 228 True Positives, 237 True Negatives, only 4 False Positives, and 11 False Negatives. The incorporation of our five novel features substantially mitigated false alarms on benign shortened collaboration links (e.g., bit.ly links redirecting to Zoom or Microsoft Teams).

The global attribution rankings in Figure 1 confirm that domain-level attributes exert the strongest influence on model classifications. The single most influential predictor is is_common_tld (mean |SHAP| = 2.18), followed by tld_length (1.30) and domain Shannon entropy (1.28). Among our engineered features, brand_impersonation_score (0.58) and homograph_spoof_score (0.41) ranked within the top ten predictors, proving that quantifying visual and typographical similarity directly enhances the model's ability to intercept targeted banking clones. Structural complexity features, including letter_digit_ratio (1.18), num_slashes (0.86), and num_subdomains (0.77), occupy the intermediate tiers.

Fig. 2 Illustration of the SHAP beeswarm plot exhibiting directional impact of feature values.

The directional beeswarm plot in Figure 2 demonstrates how feature values govern model predictions. For is_common_tld, low values (uncommon, disposable TLDs such as .xyz, .top, .buzz, and .click) exert substantial positive SHAP contributions, driving the output toward the malicious class. Conversely, higher domain entropy and longer TLD lengths correlate strongly with fraudulent domain generation algorithms. Structural features exhibit positive slopes: URLs characterized by deep directory structures, multiple subdomains, and abnormal digit-to-letter ratios are classified as malicious, whereas simpler URLs reflect legitimate architectures. Crucially, has_https displays near-zero SHAP importance (0.08), corroborating that over 76% of modern phishing campaigns leverage legitimate SSL/TLS certificates to project false security.

3.3. Real-Time Deployment & Client-Side Browser Extension

A critical limitation of prior academic literature is the absence of operational deployment. To bridge the gap between theoretical modeling and active defense, we engineered an autonomous Manifest V3 Chrome Extension. The extension operates with dual-tier interception: (1) pre-navigation click capture that intercepts hyperlinks before HTTP socket initialization; and (2) social redirect unwrapping that unpacks Meta link shims ('l.instagram.com/?u=...') to evaluate the true destination. Benign sites trigger a non-intrusive 1.0-second floating security badge, whereas detected phishing targets trigger an interstitial blocking modal presenting plain-language TreeSHAP threat drivers. Across 1,000 live navigation benchmarks, the autonomous extension achieved an average inference latency of 14.2 milliseconds, delivering real-time defense with zero perceptible impact on browsing speed.

4. Conclusion and Future Work

Deceptive URLs propagated through social media advertising remain a pervasive threat to consumer financial security. In this study, we developed PhishGuard-XAI, an advanced explainable cyber defense framework expanding upon the benchmark of Dirchaoui et al. [1]. By incorporating an expanded 2,400-URL dataset and five novel features targeting IDN homographs and brand proximity, our model achieved 96.88% Accuracy and 98.28% Precision. Furthermore, we successfully translated TreeSHAP interpretability into an operational browser extension delivering sub-15ms client-side protection.

Future research will explore integrating lightweight on-device vision-language models to perform automated visual logo verification and DOM semantic analysis, further strengthening resilience against zero-hour brand impersonation attacks.
